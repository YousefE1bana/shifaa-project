## 1. Problem and scope

### Problem statement

Patients and authorized clinicians need allergy records whose origin and verification status remain clear after correction. Clinical reviewers need a traceable publication process so draft, unsigned or unapproved material cannot be mistaken for approved decision-support content. Engineering remains synthetic-only while clinical and legal production gates are open.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-10-01

- Q: Who may create allergy reports in Feature 011? → A: Patients may report allergies for themselves. Authorized clinicians may create allergy records. Guardians and delegates do not gain `createAllergy` authority in Feature 011; preserve their existing read authority only where already authorized by the current relationship/record rules.
- Q: What initial verification status should a newly created allergy record have? → A: Every newly created allergy record starts as `self_reported`, regardless of whether the recorder is the patient or an authorized clinician. `clinician_confirmed` and `refuted` are reached only through `verifyAllergy`, producing attributable verification evidence and a new version.
- Q: How should an allergy record be marked and handled when it was entered in error? → A: Only an authorized clinician may mark `entered_in_error` through `verifyAllergy`, with a reason, attributable evidence and a new version. This state is terminal for that record. A corrected assertion must be a new record created as `self_reported`, with explicit correction/supersession linkage to the erroneous record. Retain the API Catalog's confirmed/refuted-only wording as a later planning reconciliation item without adding an operation.
- Q: Must eligible physician and pharmacist signers also hold the existing medical_reviewer (ADM-MEDICAL) admin role? → A: No. Clinical-content signing requires verified physician/pharmacist professional eligibility, explicit current signing authority, purpose, AAL2 and independent signatures; `ADM-MEDICAL` is not required. Signing alone grants no create/publish/admin authority. `createClinicalContentRelease` and `publishClinicalContentRelease` remain `ADM-MEDICAL` responsibilities.
- Q: How should a newly published clinical-content release replace an existing published release? → A: Use explicit supersession for the same governed content. A newly published release explicitly identifies the release it supersedes and becomes eligible only from its effective date. Reject ambiguous overlap, conflicting supersession chains and multiple competing successors for the same effective scope. Preserve complete prior release/signature history; future-dated releases remain ineligible until effective. Rollback is a new independently signed release explicitly superseding the currently effective release.
- Q: How should the first clinical-content release be published when no prior release exists for the same governed content? → A: Allow an explicitly identified initial release with no predecessor only for the first release of that governed content. Independent physician and pharmacist signatures, publication authorization, effective-date eligibility and all applicable retained OPEN gates remain required. Every later replacement must explicitly supersede the currently applicable release.
- Q: Which status changes should an authorized clinician be allowed to make after an allergy is clinician-confirmed or refuted? → A: Allow `clinician_confirmed` → `refuted` and `refuted` → `clinician_confirmed` only through `verifyAllergy`, with a new reason, attributable verification evidence and a new version. A verified record cannot return to `self_reported`; `entered_in_error` remains terminal. Preserve the complete verification history.
- Q: What minimum information must an allergy report contain before it can be created? → A: Require substance identity/display and source at creation; use a validated substance code when available. Coding, reaction, severity and onset may be explicitly unknown when not known. Never fabricate missing clinical values. Preserve the distinction between known values, explicitly unknown values and later clinician verification/correction.
- Q: Who may view clinical-content status in the clinic app, and what information may they see? → A: Clinic staff require explicit current status-read authority, valid current workforce/facility context, purpose and AAL2. Expose only published/effective release metadata: source, version, effective date, publication/eligibility status and supersession lineage, with minimum signature-status evidence only. No draft/unsigned details, content bodies, excess signatures, create/sign/publish authority or `ADM-MEDICAL` privileges are derived from this access. Draft/unsigned details retain separate governance/admin authority. Reconcile the catalog's current `ADM-MEDICAL`-only `listClinicalContent` wording in Plan within that existing operation; add no operation.
- Q: What evidence must establish that a signer qualifies as the required senior physician/medical director or clinical pharmacist? → A: Verified credentials plus governance designation: authoritative professional verification plus an evidence-backed clinical-owner designation for the required reviewer capacity. Unresolved eligibility denies signing; clinical qualification criteria remain subject to `OPEN-CLIN-001`.

- Q: Who determines eligible content categories, acceptable sources, and required code/test-vector evidence for Feature 011? → A: Clinical owners; synthetic fixtures only. Clinical owners define and separately approve admissibility policy; missing approval denies real-content eligibility. Engineering uses neutral synthetic fixtures while retained gates remain open.

## 3. User Scenarios & Testing _(mandatory)_

### Journey J-01 — Read and report an allergy

1. Given a patient reporting for self or a currently authorized clinician creating a patient's allergy record, with substance identity/display and source. Use a validated substance code when available; coding, reaction, severity and onset may be explicitly unknown when not known.
2. When the actor reports the allergy.
3. One record starts as `self_reported` regardless of whether the recorder is the patient or an authorized clinician, and shows recorder, source, version and time. Known values and explicitly unknown values remain distinct; no missing clinical value is fabricated. Creation does not confer clinician confirmation.
4. Only authorized patient-context readers see it. An attributable receipt confirms the result; no warning, diagnosis, prescription change or emergency-contact message occurs.

### Journey J-02 — Verify or correct without erasing history

1. Given a currently authorized clinician, existing allergy version and an approved transition from the §5 status-changing matrix.
2. When the clinician uses `verifyAllergy` to confirm, refute, revise a confirmed/refuted outcome or mark `entered_in_error`, with a new reason, attributable verification evidence and a new version.
3. A new version records verifier/outcome and preserves the prior assertion and recorder, including its original known/explicitly unknown values. Readers distinguish reported values from later attributable clinician verification/correction and current status from earlier evidence.
4. Unauthorized, stale or invalid actions have no partial effect; correction never silently deletes history. `entered_in_error` is terminal for that record. Any corrected assertion is created through `createAllergy` as a new `self_reported` record with explicit correction/supersession linkage to the erroneous record; the creator must satisfy the already-resolved creation authority.

### Journey J-03 — Independently review and publish

1. Given an authorized medical reviewer and a neutral synthetic category/source/artifact fixture. Clinical owners define and separately approve admissibility for real content; missing approval denies real-content eligibility.
2. When a draft with source/version/effective-date/artifact identity receives two independent physician and pharmacist approvals of that exact artifact, each signer having verified professional eligibility, explicit current signing authority, purpose and AAL2. Signing does not require `ADM-MEDICAL`.
3. An authorized creator-independent publisher may publish only after all approval, independence, current-version and applicable gate guards pass. Only the explicitly identified first release of that governed content may omit a predecessor; every later replacement explicitly supersedes the currently applicable release.
4. Creator/reviewer/publisher evidence remains attributable. Signing alone grants no creation/publication/admin authority; release creation and publication remain `ADM-MEDICAL` responsibilities. Synthetic mechanism-test publication is never presented as real clinical approval or production release.

### Journey J-04 — Understand unavailable or ineligible content

1. Given an appropriately authorized governance/admin viewer, or clinic staff with explicit current status-read authority, valid current workforce/facility context, purpose and AAL2.
2. When a governance/admin viewer inspects permitted draft/unsigned or publication details, or a clinic viewer requests published/effective release metadata and availability.
3. Clinic viewers receive only source, version, effective date, publication/eligibility status and supersession lineage with minimum signature-status evidence; no draft/unsigned details or bodies are exposed. Governance/admin details require their separate appropriate authority. Ineligible content has no decision effect; status-read permission grants no create/sign/publish/admin authority.
4. Missing eligible content is unavailable/unknown, never silently safe. A replacement explicitly identifies the release it supersedes for the same governed content and becomes eligible only from its effective date. Ambiguous overlap, conflicting chains and multiple competing successors for the same effective scope are rejected. Rollback creates a new independently signed release superseding the currently effective release; all prior releases and signatures remain intact.

### State machine

Allergy verification status is exactly `self_reported`, `clinician_confirmed`, `refuted`, `entered_in_error`. Every newly created record starts `self_reported`, whether recorded by the patient or an authorized clinician. Only `verifyAllergy` may produce `clinician_confirmed` or `refuted`; each successful verification produces attributable verification evidence and a new version, preserving the prior version. An authorized clinician may revise `clinician_confirmed` → `refuted` or `refuted` → `clinician_confirmed` only through `verifyAllergy`, with a new reason, attributable verification evidence and a new version; preserve the complete verification history. A verified record cannot return to `self_reported`. Only an authorized clinician may mark `entered_in_error` through `verifyAllergy`, with a reason, attributable evidence and a new version. `entered_in_error` is terminal for that allergy record: no subsequent action may change it to another status. Any corrected assertion must be created as a new `self_reported` record through `createAllergy`, with explicit correction/supersession linkage to the erroneous record. Reporting is not confirmation; refutation differs from data-entry error. Q1 creation/transition/correction/field policy is resolved below; physical representation remains a later Plan concern.

Creation requires substance identity/display and source. Use a validated substance code when available; otherwise coding may be explicitly unknown. Reaction, severity and onset may also be explicitly unknown when not known. Preserve known values, explicitly unknown values and later clinician verification/correction as distinct attributable information, including the original values in version history. Unknown is not a normal, negative or safe clinical value. Missing clinical values are never fabricated; no medical code, reaction, severity or onset is inferred to complete a report.

Release publication states in this slice are `draft` and `published`. Signature completeness is associated evidence, not an invented publication state. Creation produces draft; only publication produces published after all guards. Signing does not publish/activate. Missing/rejected approval does not create an extra release status. An explicitly identified initial release may have no predecessor only when it is the first release of that governed content. It still requires independent physician/pharmacist signatures, publication authorization, effective-date eligibility and all applicable retained OPEN gates; no predecessor is fabricated. Every later replacement explicitly supersedes the currently applicable release for the same governed content. The replacement becomes eligible only from its effective date; published future-dated releases remain ineligible until effective. Reject ambiguous overlap, conflicting supersession chains and multiple competing successors for the same effective scope. Preserve complete prior release/signature history. Supersession changes eligibility without inventing a third publication state. Rollback requires a new independently signed release that explicitly supersedes the currently effective release, using the existing creation/signing/publication operations.

### Invariants and concurrency

- Every allergy version preserves patient binding, original recorder/source and correction linkage; verification adds named evidence.
- Substance identity/display and source are required for creation. Coding uses a validated code when available; coding/reaction/severity/onset may be explicitly unknown. Known values, explicitly unknown values and later attributable clinician verification/correction remain distinguishable across history; absent clinical information is never replaced with fabricated values.
- `createAllergy` permits patients reporting for themselves and currently authorized clinicians only. Guardians/delegates retain existing authorized reads under current relationship/record rules and gain no creation or other mutation authority from this feature.
- Creation never produces `clinician_confirmed`, `refuted` or `entered_in_error`, even when recorded by a clinician. Confirmation/refutation requires `verifyAllergy`, attributable evidence and an appended version; neither creation nor a silent update substitutes for verification.
- Confirmed/refuted revisions require an authorized clinician, `verifyAllergy`, a new reason, attributable verification evidence and an appended version. Complete verification history remains intact; no verified record resets to `self_reported` and no error-marked record leaves its terminal status.
- Marking `entered_in_error` requires an authorized clinician, `verifyAllergy`, a reason, attributable evidence and an appended version. The erroneous record remains terminal; a corrected assertion has a distinct record identity, starts `self_reported`, explicitly links to the erroneous record and preserves both histories. Replacement never inherits confirmation or bypasses creation authority.
- Creator cannot approve/sign or publish their own release. Required physician and pharmacist are distinct eligible people, each independent of the creator.
- Clinical-content signing requires verified professional eligibility, explicit current signing authority, purpose, AAL2 and independent signatures; it does not require `ADM-MEDICAL`. Signing alone confers no creation/publication/admin authority. Release creation and publication require their existing `ADM-MEDICAL` authority and all other guards.
- Professional eligibility requires authoritative professional verification plus an evidence-backed clinical-owner designation for the required senior physician/medical director or clinical pharmacist reviewer capacity. Unresolved eligibility denies signing; a license, admin title or signing grant alone does not substitute for the required evidence. Clinical qualification criteria remain subject to `OPEN-CLIN-001`; synthetic fixtures neither certify a professional nor approve real content.
- Clinic status-read authority requires current workforce/facility context, purpose and AAL2 and confers only the published/effective metadata projection with minimum signature-status evidence. It does not confer draft/unsigned details, content bodies, additional signature evidence, create/sign/publish authority or `ADM-MEDICAL` privileges; governance/admin inspection uses separately appropriate authority.
- Approval binds exact resource/version/artifact and an approving decision; changed content cannot reuse another version/digest's signatures.
- Content replacement preserves explicit same-content supersession lineage and complete prior release/signature history. Publication and eligibility reject ambiguous overlap, conflicting chains and competing successors for the same effective scope; concurrent publication cannot produce competing accepted successors. Future-dated publication does not confer current eligibility. Rollback follows normal independent approval/publication for a new release superseding the currently effective release, never an in-place history rewrite or old-signature replay.
- No-predecessor publication is valid only for the explicitly identified first release of a governed content identity and bypasses none of the signature, authorization, effective-date or applicable OPEN-gate requirements. A later release cannot use the initial-release exception to evade supersession; concurrent attempts cannot produce multiple accepted initial releases for the same governed content.
- Clinical owners define and separately approve real-content categories, acceptable source provenance and associated code/test-vector evidence within the frozen Feature 011 scope. Missing admissibility approval denies real-content eligibility; engineering uses neutral synthetic fixtures only. No medical rules, source allowlist, clinical qualification criteria or real approval are fabricated, and no retained gate is closed.
- Draft, unsigned, mismatched, unavailable or policy-ineligible content has zero decision effect. Published status alone is not clinical or production approval.
- Races must not lose corrections, combine signatures across versions, bypass live revocation or duplicate publication. A successful mutation records its complete attributable outcome; failures leave no partial domain effect.
- Existing replay/version-conflict rules apply; no new operation, unapproved transition or deletion shortcut resolves a conflict.
